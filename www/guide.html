How to Use RPN Field Brain

A complete guide to getting the most out of this app — from first setup through to using it in a real emergency.

1. Getting Started — First Time Setup

Step 1 — Set up your household. Go to More → Household and enter how many adults, children and pets you have. Set realistic daily calorie and water targets. This is the foundation of the whole app — it drives the "Days of Food" and "Days of Water" numbers on the home screen. Without this, those numbers will be wrong.


Step 2 — Add your stockpile. Go to Stockpile and start entering what you have. For food items, enter the weight in grams so the app can calculate how many days of calories it provides. Add expiry dates — the app will warn you when things are going out of date. Use the Location field so you know exactly where everything is stored.


Step 3 — Work through the checklists. Go to Lists and open each checklist relevant to you. Tick off what you have. The home screen shows your overall percentage complete — aim for 100% on the checklists that matter most to your situation.


Step 4 — Save the map for offline. Go to Map, navigate to your local area, zoom in to street level, and tap ⇓ Offline. Do this for your home area, your bug-out route, and any meetup points. Once saved, the map works without internet.


Step 5 — Add your radio frequencies. If you have a radio, go to More → Radio Notebook and store your key frequencies, repeaters, and net schedules. This is your communications backup — make sure it is filled in while you have time, not during an emergency.

2. In an Emergency — What to Do

Start with Scenarios. The large amber button on the home screen takes you straight to the Scenario Engine. Pick the situation closest to what is happening. The app will walk you through it step by step — asking questions, giving actions, and pointing you to relevant checklists. Do not try to remember everything. Follow the steps.


Use the Knowledge library. Go to Knowledge for offline reference guides — first aid, water purification, fire, navigation, communications and more. These are available without internet. The medical guides follow ERC 2021 and NHS guidelines. They are a reference tool, not a replacement for proper training.


Use the Map. Open Map to see your saved pins — rally points, caches, hazards, people's locations. Toggle layers on and off to reduce clutter. If you saved the map tiles for your area while online, the full street map will be available offline. Drop new pins to mark things as the situation develops.


Check your stockpile. Go to Stockpile to see what you have and where it is stored. Red items have expired — do not use them. The Days of Food and Days of Water figures tell you how long your current supplies will last.


Communications. If mobile networks are down, go to More → Radio Notebook for your saved frequencies and net schedules. If you have MeshCore devices, go to More → Mesh Network to check your channel settings and contacts.

3. Staying Prepared — Maintaining the App

Check expiry dates regularly. Open the Stockpile once a month. Amber items expire within 30 days — rotate them into your normal food supply and replace them. Red items have already expired — remove and replace. The home screen shows a count of items needing attention so you know at a glance.


Update your stockpile as things change. Every time you buy new supplies, add them. Every time you use something, remove it or reduce the quantity. The Days of Food and Water figures are only useful if the data is accurate.


Keep checklists current. If your situation changes — new family members, new equipment, new location — revisit the checklists. Create custom checklists for things specific to your setup.


Run your radio nets. Regular radio contact builds the habits and relationships you will need when things go wrong. Log your nets in the Radio Notebook. Check your equipment is working. Know your repeaters.


Back up your data. Go to More → Export Backup regularly and save the file somewhere safe — another device, a USB drive, printed out. All your stockpile, radio, map and household data is in that file. The file is called rpn-field-brain-backup.json.


Restoring from a backup. If you need to move the app to a new device, go to More and scroll down to Restore from Backup. Select your saved rpn-field-brain-backup.json file and tap Restore. The app will confirm before overwriting — once restored it takes you straight back to the home screen with all your data in place.

4. Offline Use

Most of the app works offline already. Scenarios, Checklists, Stockpile, Knowledge, Radio Notebook, Mesh Network, Household and all your Map pins are stored locally on the device. They do not need internet to load or function.


The map background needs to be saved in advance. The street map tiles are downloaded from OpenStreetMap when you are online. To use them offline you must save them first. Navigate to each area you care about, zoom in to the detail level you need, and tap ⇓ Offline. Areas you have not saved will appear grey when offline — your pins will still be there, just without the map underneath.


Export your backup before things go wrong. Keep a copy of your exported backup file on a USB drive or another device. If the device is lost or wiped, you can install the app fresh and restore all your data.

5. RPN Community Features

Joining the RPN Community. Go to More → RPN Community and tap Create Account. Enter your name, email, and password. Once registered you will have access to the community features. Your RPN callsign is issued by the admin team — you will be notified when it is assigned.


The Community Map. Once signed in, the main map shows community pins placed by RPN members — rally points, On Air markers, MeshCore repeaters, and portable companion nodes. You can add your own community pins that are visible to all members. Only you can edit or delete your own pins.


On Air pins. Going on air? Drop an On Air pin so other members can see you are active. Set your channel, and the pin expires automatically when you set it — no cleanup needed.


The Logbook. Go to Logbook to record your radio contacts. Log the callsign, RST report, frequency, and mode. If the contact is another RPN member and you both want to, you can share the contact live on the map — a line appears between your two locations so the community can see active contacts in real time. The other member receives a notification and can accept or decline.


Noticeboard. Go to More → Noticeboard for announcements from the RPN team. New announcements are pushed to your device as notifications.


Your profile. Go to More → RPN Community when signed in to update your profile — name, region, radio equipment, and bio. Your profile is visible to other RPN members.

6. Legal & Data

RPN Field Brain is pre-release software. Access is confidential. By using this app you have agreed not to share or distribute it without written permission from Radio Prepper Network.


The medical and first aid content is for reference only and is based on ERC 2021, NHS and Red Cross guidelines. It is not a substitute for professional medical training. Always call 112 in a medical emergency.


Local data — your stockpile, checklists, map pins, radio notebook, household data, and drills are stored on your device only and never transmitted.


Community data — if you create an RPN Community account, your profile, community map pins, logbook entries you choose to share, and push notification token are stored on the RPN server. This data is used solely to power the community features and is not shared with any third party.

7. Drills — Practise Before You Need To

What are drills? Drills are timed, step-by-step exercises you run in real life. Not reading — actually doing. The app gives you a timer, a list of steps to complete, and logs the result so you can track your improvement over time.


Finding drills. Go to More â†' Drills or tap Drills on the home screen. Built-in drills cover Bug Out, Blackout, Radio Check, Bleeding Control, Family Comms, Water Supply, Full Comms Blackout, and Vehicle Readiness.


Running a drill. Tap a drill card then tap Run Drill. A timer starts automatically when you tick your first step. Work through the steps in real life, tapping each one as you complete it. When you are done tap Finish to see your results — time taken, steps missed, and a rating of Ready, Needs Work, or Failed.


Personalising a drill. Every built-in drill is fully editable. Tap Edit on any drill card to customise the steps, change the target time, or reword steps to match your exact setup. For example change "Grab your bug-out bag from its stored location" to "Grab bag from garage shelf 2." Your changes are saved as a personal override — the original template is never deleted.


Creating a custom drill. Tap + Custom Drill at the top of the Drills page to build one from scratch. Name it, write your own steps, set your own target time. Use this for drills specific to your situation — your bug-out route, your radio net, your family plan.


Adding notes after a drill. On the results screen there is a notes field. Use it to record what went wrong, what you need to buy, or what to brief your family on. These notes are saved in your drill log.


The drill log. Every completed drill is saved to your log with the date, time taken, steps completed, rating, and notes. Over time this shows your improvement — a bug-out drill that took 18 minutes last month and 11 minutes this month is real, measurable progress. Aim to run each drill at least once a month.


Tip. Run drills unannounced where possible — especially the Family Comms Plan drill. The whole point is to test what your household actually knows, not what they can look up.

8. MeshCore & Off-Grid Communications

What is MeshCore? MeshCore is a radio mesh network that runs on small, cheap LoRa devices. Each device acts as a node — messages and GPS positions hop between nodes without any internet, mobile signal, or infrastructure. It works when everything else has gone down.


How it fits with Field Brain. RPN Field Brain is your preparation and community hub — stockpile, scenarios, knowledge, and the RPN member map. MeshCore is your off-grid radio layer — it keeps your team connected when you cannot use your phone as a phone. The two work alongside each other, not instead of each other.


MeshCore SAR. MeshCore has a dedicated Search and Rescue mode called MeshCore SAR. It turns your phone into a tactical coordination terminal for a search operation — GPS tracking of team members, geo-tagged markers (person found, fire, staging area), offline maps with route drawing, and direct messaging, all over the MeshCore mesh with no internet required. If you are involved in any kind of organised search, rescue, or response operation, MeshCore SAR is the tool to use. You can read more at dz0ny.dev/posts/meshcore-sar.


Repeater and Companion pins. On the RPN community map in Field Brain you can drop two types of MeshCore pin. A Repeater pin (purple) marks a permanent MeshCore node or repeater at a fixed location — useful for the community to see coverage. A Companion pin (teal) marks a portable node you have set up temporarily — it expires automatically. Use these to build up a shared picture of mesh coverage in your area.


Getting started with MeshCore. You need at least one LoRa device — the HTCC-AB02S and Heltec V3 are commonly used in the RPN community. Channels and node settings are saved in More → Mesh Network in Field Brain so you always have your config to hand offline. Coordinate your channel names and frequencies with your local RPN group so you can all reach each other.
